\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

Material conversion exposes a gap between stable aggregate performance and
stable decisions. In navigation, a net loss of four successful episodes hides
30 changes in success status. In grounding, equal hit totals can likewise
conceal different successful queries. Paired outcomes reveal this redistribution
directly.

The grounding experiments also show why interpreting that redistribution
requires care with the measurement itself. Coordinate conventions determine how
a response becomes a location; query-position swaps test whether that location
follows the requested object; region crossovers distinguish dependence on the
target window from dependence on its surroundings. These comparisons turn a
before/after score into a more informative account of model behavior.

As simulated scenes move between rendering systems, their geometry alone does
not specify the evidence an embodied model will receive. Tracking which
decisions change, alongside how much the mean changes, makes material
representation an explicit and testable part of embodied evaluation.
